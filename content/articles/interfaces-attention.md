# On making interfaces that reward attention

Design should not always accelerate us. Over the past decade, software design has worshiped frictionlessness. We measure success by seconds saved, conversions expedited, and cognitive overhead eliminated.

Yet in the rush to remove friction, we have also eliminated depth. When an interaction requires no consideration, it leaves no residue in memory.

## The Cost of Velocity

When every interface behaves like a slide, users never encounter edges they can grasp. We consume, dismiss, and forget. The modern web often feels like a series of frictionless ramps leading directly into amnesia.

What would it look like to design software that invites contemplation?

> “A tool that speeds up every thought will inevitably train you to only have thoughts that can move quickly.”

## Three Principles for Slow Interfaces

1. **Spatial Stability**: Things should stay where you put them. The constant shifting of feeds and dynamic reorganizations destabilizes the user's mental model.
2. **Intentional Thresholds**: Pauses before irreversible actions that invite reflection rather than reflex clicks.
3. **Calm Typography**: High-contrast, well-proportioned editorial hierarchy that respects reading cadence instead of competing for glance value.
