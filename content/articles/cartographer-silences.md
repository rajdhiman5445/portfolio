# The Cartographer of Small Silences

By morning, every map in the archive had grown a new coastline.

Mara found them while opening the western drawers: thin black lines curling through familiar cities, crossing avenues and bedrooms, dividing kitchens from their windows. The ink was still wet.

She stood very still. The archive held eleven thousand maps, each printed, folded, and catalogued by hand. Nothing inside it was supposed to change.

Outside, the city was waking without incident. Trains carried their small bright rooms through tunnels. A baker lifted the shutters across the street. Somewhere, a kettle began to whistle and was forgotten.

> “A map,” her teacher once told her, “is simply an argument about what deserves to remain visible.”

Mara placed the oldest map beneath the examination lamp. The new coastline passed directly through the house where she had grown up.

Where the living room used to be, the chart indicated three fathoms of water.
